## Wall Connector Overview

The Tesla Wall Connector is the recommended home charging solution. The current generation delivers up to 11.5 kilowatts at 48 amps, which adds up to 44 miles of range per hour. It has a 24-foot cable and currently sells for 475 dollars.

The Wall Connector carries a 4-year warranty when installed in a residential application.

## Wall Connector Installation Requirements

Installation must be performed by a qualified licensed electrician and typically takes a few hours.

To obtain the full 48 amps of output, a 60-amp circuit breaker is required. Electrical code requires continuous loads such as EV charging to draw no more than 80 percent of a circuit's rated capacity, and 48 amps is 80 percent of 60 amps.

The Wall Connector should not be connected to a GFCI breaker because it has ground fault protection built in.

The unit automatically adjusts its amperage based on the circuit the electrician installs. During commissioning the installer must set the maximum current to match the electrical system. Leaving the default 48-amp setting on a circuit that cannot support it is the most common installation problem.

## Onboard Charger Limits by Model

The vehicle's onboard charger sets a ceiling that no wall unit, cable, or app setting can exceed. The lower of what the vehicle accepts and what the circuit delivers determines the actual charging speed.

Model 3 Rear-Wheel Drive, Model Y Rear-Wheel Drive, and Model Y All-Wheel Drive have a 32-amp, 7.7-kilowatt onboard charger limit. These vehicles charge at up to 30 miles per hour regardless of Wall Connector output.

To check a specific vehicle's limit, tap the lightning bolt icon on the touchscreen.

Sizing the installation around the specific trim avoids paying an electrician for breaker capacity the vehicle cannot use.

## Mobile Connector

The Mobile Connector is better suited to travel and to renters than to primary daily charging. The second generation maxes out at 32 amps.

The current bundle includes a 20-foot Mobile Connector, a NEMA 5-15 adapter, a NEMA 14-50 adapter, and a cable bag.

A standard 120-volt household outlet provides up to 3 miles of range per hour. A 240-volt outlet provides up to 30 miles of range per hour depending on the model.

## Mobile Connector Charging Speed by Model

Using a NEMA 5-15 outlet at 120 volts and 12 amps, range gained per hour is approximately 6 miles for Model 3, 5 miles for Model S, 4 miles for Model X, and 5 miles for Model Y.

Using a NEMA 14-50 or NEMA 6-50 outlet at 240 volts and 32 amps, range gained per hour is approximately 30 miles for Model 3, 27 miles for Model S, 23 miles for Model X, and 27 miles for Model Y.

## Mobile Connector Safety Features

Tesla uses a smart adapter system with an embedded resistor that identifies the outlet type before charging begins and sets the maximum safe current automatically.

The adapters include temperature monitoring. If an outlet overheats due to poor wiring or a loose connection, the charger reduces power or stops charging entirely.

## Choosing Between Home Charging Options

For drivers who charge overnight and travel less than 30 to 40 miles per day, 120-volt charging with the Mobile Connector typically meets daily needs.

At the Wall Connector's 44 miles per hour, most Tesla models charge fully in 6 to 12 hours depending on battery size.

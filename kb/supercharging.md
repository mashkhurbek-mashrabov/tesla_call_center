## Supercharger Pricing

There is no single national Supercharger rate. Pricing varies by site and frequently by time of day. Typical United States pricing falls in the range of 30 to 50 cents per kilowatt-hour, with off-peak hours generally cheaper.

Current pricing for any specific Supercharger location is shown on the vehicle touchscreen and in the Tesla app before a session begins.

Billing is per kilowatt-hour in most regions. Charges are applied to the payment method on file in the Tesla Account and appear in the app's charging history.

## Idle Fees

An idle fee is charged when a vehicle has finished charging but remains plugged in and continues to occupy a Supercharger stall.

Idle fees run approximately 50 cents to 1 dollar per minute in the United States and can reach 1 Canadian dollar or more per minute in Canada.

Idle fees only apply when at least half of the stalls at the site are occupied. The fee doubles when the station reaches 100 percent occupancy. The fee stops the moment the vehicle is moved.

The Tesla app sends a notification when the vehicle reaches the threshold, and the idle fee is waived if the vehicle is moved within five minutes.

## Congestion Fees

A congestion fee is a separate charge that begins once the vehicle reaches 80 percent state of charge, whether or not it is still actively charging.

The congestion fee applies 5 minutes after the vehicle hits 80 percent displayed state of charge, or when the session ends if the charge limit is set below 80 percent. The rate is a flat 50 cents per minute at sites Tesla has designated as busy.

Congestion fees were introduced in 2023 and Tesla has been transitioning sites from idle fees to congestion fees. Most United States locations and most countries now use congestion fees. A given site uses one structure or the other, not both.

The rationale is that the last 20 percent of a charge is the slowest part of the charging curve, so a vehicle charging from 80 to 100 percent occupies a stall for a long time while gaining relatively few miles.

## How to Avoid Idle and Congestion Fees

Move the vehicle within five minutes of the notification to have the idle fee waived.

At busy sites during road trips, set the charge limit below 80 percent so the session ends before the congestion window opens.

Check the vehicle touchscreen or the Tesla app at the start of each session. It indicates whether fees are currently in effect at that location.

## Free Supercharging and Fee Edge Cases

Vehicles with free unlimited Supercharging that were purchased and delivered before 2017 do not incur a congestion fee during an active charging session. Fees do apply if the site is busy and the battery is already at or above 80 percent.

Vehicles using free Supercharging credits can be charged a congestion fee even during an active charging session.

## Supercharger Network and Charging Speed

Tesla vehicles use a native NACS connector and plug directly into the Supercharger network without an adapter. The United States network has roughly 38,700 Supercharger stalls.

Maximum DC fast-charging rates are up to 250 kilowatts for Premium, Performance, and Model Y L configurations, and up to 225 kilowatts for Standard trims.

## How Software Updates Work

Tesla vehicles receive software updates over the air at no charge. Updates add features, improve existing functionality, and deliver fixes without a Service Center visit.

Updates download over Wi-Fi or, for some smaller updates, over the vehicle's cellular connection. Connecting the vehicle to Wi-Fi at home significantly increases how promptly updates arrive.

## Installing an Update

When an update is ready, a notification appears on the vehicle touchscreen and in the Tesla app. Tap the notification to schedule installation immediately or for a later time.

The vehicle cannot be driven while an update is installing. Installation typically takes 20 to 30 minutes, though larger updates take longer.

Updates can also be started from the Tesla app under Software.

## Update Preferences

Software update preferences are set on the vehicle touchscreen under Controls, then Software. The Standard setting delivers updates after broader release. The Advanced setting delivers updates sooner, as soon as they are available for that vehicle.

Choosing Advanced does not enroll the vehicle in an early access program, and update availability still depends on vehicle configuration and region.

## Why an Update May Not Appear

Update rollout is staged by region, vehicle model, and configuration, so two vehicles in the same household may receive the same update at different times.

Common reasons an update has not arrived include the vehicle not being connected to Wi-Fi, the update not yet being released for that vehicle's configuration, or an open service issue that blocks the rollout.

Vehicles that have been transported outside their original market may receive a limited set of features and updates.

## Release Notes

Release notes for the currently installed version are available on the vehicle touchscreen by tapping the Tesla T icon, then Release Notes. They can also be viewed in the Tesla app.
